**WIP prototype. Not ready for use. Do not use for real funds or production puzzles.**

Some work does not fit in one spend. This original teaching policy for
[#16](https://github.com/judeallred/chialisp-oxide/issues/16) pays a committed,
ordered list of recipients one per spend. Each spend recreates the same coin
for the rest of the queue, so no spender can skip, reorder, inject or end early.

## Read the policy in three languages

<SourceComparison slug="ordered-payout" />

The export uses the facade’s `chialisp_oxide::continuation` helpers:

- `queue_item(domain, recipient, next)` and `queue_end(domain)` build the
  queue’s hash chain;
- `list_hash_2` hashes the curried `(module head)` list;
- `self_curried(module, terms_hash)` gives the successor’s puzzle hash.

They are ordinary functions over the reviewed SHA bridge, not compiler
intrinsics, and they choose no policy. Order, funds and termination stay
explicit in the export.

## Let consensus count the remaining work

A funded instance curries only its own module hash and the digest of the
remaining queue. The spender supplies `(RECIPIENT NEXT MY_AMOUNT)`, and the
policy emits `ASSERT_MY_AMOUNT(MY_AMOUNT)`, so consensus checks the amount
instead of the puzzle trusting it:

- the opening must hash to the committed head;
- the final step (`NEXT` is the terminal digest) requires exactly one 1,000-mojo
  payout and creates no successor;
- a nonterminal step requires at least two payouts and recreates the module
  curried with `NEXT` and the amount minus one payout.

Because the budget is the coin’s value, the curried state holds no counter and
the queue needs no on-chain length table. The host must check before funding
that the coin holds one payout per recipient and that the module field names
the selected artifact; a mis-funded instance can become permanently stuck.

## Advance a real chain

The corpus funds an actual self-curried instance in the SDK simulator for the
Rust, Chialisp and Rue builds. Three steps pay every recipient in order across
mined blocks. A misstated amount with a valid opening fails with
`AssertMyAmountFailed`, and replaying a consumed step fails with `DoubleSpend`;
each rejected bundle leaves the simulator unchanged.

Advancement is permissionless: a third party can race a valid step but cannot
change the recipients, order or amounts. Nothing guarantees that anyone
advances the queue, inclusion or cross-block atomicity, and these finite
observations are not a compiler-preservation proof.
