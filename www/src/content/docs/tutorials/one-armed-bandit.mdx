This original teaching game for
[#20](https://github.com/judeallred/chialisp-oxide/issues/20) is one pull of a
slot machine. Two parties each hold half of the randomness, and the timing rules
make withholding cost the house rather than the player.

## The house commits before the player picks

1. The house picks a fresh random 32-byte seed and publishes only
   `sha256("chialisp-oxide/bandit/v1/seed:00" || seed)`.
2. The player then picks a salt. One pull coin curries
   `(HOUSE_PAYOUT SEED_COMMITMENT PLAYER_PAYOUT SALT)` and holds exactly 8,000
   mojos: the player’s 1,000-mojo bet plus the house’s 7,000-mojo reserve.
3. Before the coin is ten blocks old, the house resolves by revealing the seed.
   The outcome byte is `sha256(spin domain || seed || salt)[31]`.

| Outcome byte | Chance | Player receives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below 16 | 16 / 256 | 7 bets (7,000 mojos) |
| 16 to 63 | 48 / 256 | 2 bets (2,000 mojos) |
| 64 and above | 192 / 256 | nothing |

The house receives the rest, which is always at least one bet, so a pull can
never bankrupt it. The expected return to the player is exactly
(16 × 7 + 48 × 2) / 256 = 208 / 256 = 81.25%. A Rust unit test computes that
number from the published constants over all 256 outcomes. It is exact
arithmetic, not sampling.

## Withholding costs the house

If the house sees a losing reveal coming and refuses to resolve, the player
waits ten blocks and claims all 8,000 mojos. Unlike the
[raffle](/chialisp-oxide/tutorials/raffle/), where a withholder just forces a
refund, here withholding forfeits the house’s reserve.

The corpus plays this in the SDK simulator for the Rust, Chialisp and Rue
builds:

- three resolved pulls pay a loss, a pair and a jackpot across blocks while the
  house bankroll is tracked;
- an early timeout claim fails with `AssertHeightRelativeFailed`, a misfunded
  pull fails with `AssertMyAmountFailed`, and a late resolve fails with
  `AssertBeforeHeightRelativeFailed`;
- a withholding house forfeits the whole pull to the player, and a replay fails
  with `DoubleSpend`.

Each rejected bundle leaves the simulator unchanged.

## What still requires trust

- **Fresh seeds.** If the house ever reuses a seed, a player who saw it can pick
  a winning salt. The puzzle cannot detect reuse.
- **Order of commitment.** The player must pick the salt only after the
  commitment is published, and both are fixed when the pull coin is created.
- **Scope.** There is one fixed bet and paytable, and no bankroll singleton or
  house close-out. SHA-256 is an assumption, and these finite observations are
  not a fairness proof.
