`FIRST_RECIPIENT` receives `first_amount`; `SECOND_RECIPIENT` receives
the rest of `COIN_AMOUNT`. Both destination hashes are fixed. The caller selects
the split, and the input amount is checked by a consensus condition.

## Why the guard comes before subtraction

`Amount::from_mojos` makes the unit explicit, `.is_at_most(COIN_AMOUNT)` names
the cap check, and `.mojos()` exposes the integers for checked subtraction.
The result is wrapped as an `Amount` before `create_coin` receives it.
`SplitConditions` names the result instead of repeating its tuple layout.

Rust's checked subtraction must reject when `first_amount` exceeds the
1,000-mojo `COIN_AMOUNT`. The explicit
assertion makes the intended policy readable and gives formal specifications a
clear predicate. Even if the assertion were accidentally removed, checked
integer arithmetic must not silently wrap or become a negative CLVM amount.

For a valid input, the outputs sum to exactly 1,000. A transaction-level
conservation check alone would not prove the two intended recipients receive
the correct amounts: a different recipient or an unintended fee could still
conserve value. The corpus checks exact recipient hashes and amounts.

## Exercise the extremes

At zero, the first output has amount zero and the second has amount 1,000.
At 1,000, the allocation reverses. At 1,001, policy and subtraction are outside
the accepted range. The manifest and generated-input sweep compare both Rust
backends against native execution and the independently compiled reference.

```sh
cargo oxide build --manifest-path puzzles/Cargo.toml --locked
./scripts/oxide validate --output corpus/validation.json
```

Select `oxide_puzzles::split_payment::puzzle` in the Cargo bundle manifest.
Read [the split-payment report](/chialisp-oxide/lab/split-payment/) for the
actual accepted/rejected vectors, artifacts, and simulator checks. These
destinations are fixed teaching values; choosing real recipients requires
explicit hash handling and tests, not substituting address text into the byte
array.
