## Validate the solution, not accounts

The Solana example checks that the accounts it received are what it expects:
the right owner, signer status and so on, because a caller can pass any account.

A Chia puzzle has the same problem with a different shape: its whole solution is
an untrusted CLVM tree chosen by the spender. chialisp-oxide’s default typed ABI
decodes that tree strictly. Here `amount: Amount` must be a canonical,
nonnegative integer that fits a `u64`, with no padding, before the function body
runs, and the body then checks the policy (`amount` at most the cap). Malformed,
padded or oversized solutions are rejected, and the corpus vectors exercise
them.

Strict decoding is not a policy. This export lets the caller choose any amount up
to the cap, and the unpaid remainder becomes a fee. See
[validate untrusted inputs](/chialisp-oxide/tutorials/input-validation/) for
more.
